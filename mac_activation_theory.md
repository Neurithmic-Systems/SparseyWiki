Sparsey runs in three modes, learning, recogition, and recall.  The latter two are two different kinds of retrieval.  Retrieval is also referred to as inference. That is, when a model retrieves an answer, it can essentially be viewed as making an inference, i.e., delivering its best guess given an input query.  In the case of recognition, the input query is some particular input, X, and the answer given by the model consists in activating the code (memory trace, internal representation) of X that will have been assigned at some earlier time, e.g., on a previous presentation, i.e., a learning trial, of X.  But the query, or test, presentation of X, might in general not be exactly the same as X, but just similar to X.  In the case of recall, the query is generally some portion of a previously presented input, e.g., the first few frames of a sequence, and the answer inference given by the model is the sequential activation of the remaining frames of the sequence in their correct order.  In the current implementation, any given run of the model is in exactly one of these three modes.  However, these modes can in principle be switched between rapidly and on-line, even in the middle of individual input sequences, depending on conditions in the input stream or in control signals that are separate from the sensory input stream iself.  We intend to generalize Sparsey to that switching between modes can be done on-line.  The overarching concept here is to emulate human experience more closely.  That is, a human is an autonomous agent operating in a physical world. In general, no other entity can mandate whether, at any given moment, a human brain is learning something new or just outputting previously learned knowledge.  For example, at one instant, a human might be functioning is something close to a pure retrieval (inference) mode, e.g., walking to work along the same path that has been walked a thousand other times.  But at the next instant, some wildly different event occurs, e.g., Santa's sleigh zips by right over his head.  At that instant, his brain flips into learning mode.  That is, the sensory inputs of that event are stored on-line, in that moment. And just as rapidly, the mode might switch back to retrieval mode as the human continues outputting the previously learned actions (sc motor responses are also a form of innference) that carry them along the path to work.
